Case study 03 · Dark fantasy romance

The Witch Who Couldn't Love

A moody black and white cover and an act-based interior for a dark fantasy romance, designed to sit with confidence next to the bestsellers in one of Amazon's most competitive genres.

In short

Sulaimon Qudus designed the cover and formatted the interior of The Witch Who Couldn't Love by Kamisrg Kamila, a dark fantasy romance. The work includes a full print wrap, act divider pages, numbered chapter openers and a styled table of contents.

01 / The challengeWhat the book needed

Dark romance readers know their genre very well. They can tell in a second when a cover does not belong, and they will scroll straight past it.

This book had to say dark, magical and romantic at first glance. The inside had to carry the same mood, so the reading experience did not drop from a striking cover into a plain document.

Readers: Readers of dark romance and romantasy.
Tools: InDesign, Photoshop, Canva, Word.

02 / The approachHow it was designed

  1. A monochrome cover with presence

    A hooded witch in a misty forest, in a cold desaturated palette. The title is set in a heavy condensed type, THE WITCH stacked over WHO COULDN'T LOVE, so it hits hard even at thumbnail size.

  2. Genre signals in the tagline

    The subtitle, A Dark Fantasy Romance Where Love Is the Ultimate Weakness, tells the reader exactly what they are getting and helps the right readers pick it up.

  3. Interior structured in acts

    The story is split into acts with a dark navy act divider page, followed by numbered chapter openers. It gives the book a cinematic rhythm that suits the genre.

  4. Front matter that matches the cover

    The title page and copyright page carry the same tone as the cover, so the book feels designed from the first page.

  5. A full wrap for print

    Front, spine and back cover designed as one piece, with the back cover blurb and barcode area set up for printing.

05 / The outcomeWhere the book is now

The result is a book that looks like it belongs in its genre, inside and out. The cover works at full size and as a small thumbnail, and the act structure gives readers a satisfying sense of progress.

It also shows range. The same author name appears on The Cat Who Saved a Queen, a bright children's picture book. Two very different books, each designed to fit its own readers.

06 / For your bookWhat you can take from this

Genre readers judge fast. The cover has to speak their language before they read a single word.

Before you design a fiction cover, study the top sellers in your exact sub-genre. Your cover should feel like it belongs there, while still being clearly yours.
