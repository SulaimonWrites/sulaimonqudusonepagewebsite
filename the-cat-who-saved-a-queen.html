Case study 01 · Children's picture book

The Cat Who Saved a Queen

A full visual build for a debut children's picture book. Illustrated spreads, a redesigned cover, page layouts tuned for print and Amazon A+ Content that sells the book before a parent even opens it.

In short

Sulaimon Qudus illustrated, laid out and redesigned the cover for The Cat Who Saved a Queen by Kamila Tutak, then built a four-part Amazon A+ Content set. The finished book is printed and being read by children at home.

01 / The challengeWhat the book needed

A picture book has two jobs. It has to win the parent who sees a small thumbnail on Amazon, and it has to hold the child who turns the pages at bedtime.

Kamila had a warm story about Emily, a kind little girl, and Queenie, the cat who always knows when Emily needs her most. Together they learn how love, courage and friendship can bring comfort on hard days. The story was ready. What it needed was pictures with the same heart, a cover that stands out in a crowded category, and a product page that explains the value of the book to parents in seconds.

Readers: Children aged 4 to 8 and their parents.
Tools: InDesign, Photoshop, Canva, Word.

02 / The approachHow it was designed

  1. Characters that stay the same on every page

    Emily and Queenie were designed first, so children recognise them instantly from the cover to the last spread. Consistent faces, outfits and colours make the story easy for young readers to follow.

  2. Scenes with a real sense of place

    London streets, a red telephone box, a palace guarded by knights and soft night time city scenes. Colour moves from bright daytime to calmer evening tones so the art follows the feeling of the story.

  3. Text that reads well on top of art

    Every spread was planned with calm space for the words. Text sits where it is easy to read aloud, never fighting with a face or a key detail in the picture.

  4. A cover redesign built for the thumbnail

    The hug between Emily and Queenie became the hero of the cover, with a bright garden scene and a bold, friendly title that stays readable when it shrinks to a small Amazon thumbnail.

  5. Amazon A+ Content that answers a parent's questions

    Four modules: a banner that introduces Queenie, a feature grid (A Friend Every Child Will Love, Big Feelings Gentle Words, Brave Little Heroes, Read Then Colour), an About the Author panel, and a closing module with a clear call to buy.

04 / DeliverablesWhat was delivered

  • Full colour interior illustrations
  • Print-ready interior page layout
  • Redesigned front cover, spine and back cover
  • Four Amazon A+ Content modules
  • 3D mockups for social media and promotion
  • Colouring page extras to extend the story

05 / The outcomeWhere the book is now

The book is printed and in children's hands. The photo in the gallery came from a reader's home: a young girl turning the illustrated spreads on her own. That moment is the whole point of a picture book.

On Amazon, the A+ Content gives parents the answers they look for before buying: who the characters are, what the book teaches, who wrote it, and why their child will love it.

06 / For your bookWhat you can take from this

From a design file to a child's hands. That is the only finish line that matters for a picture book.

If you are writing a children's book, plan the illustrations, the page layout and the cover together. When one person handles all three, the characters stay consistent and the book feels whole.
